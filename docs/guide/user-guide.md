## What wordavi does

wordavi helps you get fast and confident with numbers in Spanish — the kind you
actually need at a till, on a price tag, or weighing produce. It is not a general
Spanish course. It practises one skill: hearing or reading a number and knowing
what it means, and going the other way too.

Open [wordavi.com](https://wordavi.com) and you can start straight away. There is
no account, no sign-up, nothing to pay. Your progress lives on your own device.

## Installing it on your phone

The first thing onboarding offers is installing wordavi as an app. It is worth
doing: an installed copy opens from your home screen, fills the whole screen, and
works with no internet at all.

How it looks depends on your browser. Chrome and Edge show a real install button.
On an iPhone, use the Share button and then "Add to Home Screen". Brave and
Firefox keep it in the browser menu instead, so the app tells you where to look.
If you would rather not install, "continue in the browser" skips the step and
everything still works.

You can install later from Settings at any time, and the invitation on the home
screen disappears once the app is installed.

## The six ways to practise

| Mode | What you see | What you do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Number → Spanish | a numeral like `475` | write it out in Spanish |
| Spanish → number | Spanish words | type the digits |
| Listening | a speaker button | listen, then type the digits |
| Multiple choice | a numeral | tap the right one of four |
| Say it out loud | a numeral | say it; the app listens |
| Prices and weights | a price tag or a weight | type what a cashier would say |

The big orange button plays a **mixed round** drawn from every mode your device
can run — the best way to practise once the individual modes feel comfortable.
Tapping a mode row instead plays only that mode.

Two modes depend on your device. **Listening** needs a Spanish voice installed;
**Say it out loud** needs speech recognition and an internet connection. When
either is missing, that row explains itself instead of failing, and the other
modes are unaffected.

## A round, start to finish

A round is 10, 20 or 30 questions, or endless if you would rather stop when you
feel like it. You choose in Settings.

Answer, and the app tells you immediately whether it was right. Get something
wrong and it shows you the correct form before moving on — the point is to learn
the form, not to protect your score.

**Accents are forgiven.** Writing `dieciseis` instead of `dieciséis` counts as
"almost": it scores 8 out of 10, keeps your combo alive, and shows you the accent
you missed. A genuinely wrong form — `veinte y uno` instead of `veintiuno` — is
marked wrong.

**Several phrasings are accepted.** For `4,75 €`, all of "cuatro con setenta y
cinco", "cuatro euros setenta y cinco" and "cuatro setenta y cinco" are right,
because all three are things people say. For weights, `medio kilo` and
`500 gramos` are both accepted, and the app points out that they are the same
amount.

You can leave a round at any point. When you come back, the home screen offers to
continue exactly where you stopped.

At the end you get a summary: your score, the answers you got wrong first — that
is the part worth reading — and a button to retry just those. The retry counts for
real, and chains until you get them all.

## What comes back, and why

wordavi keeps track of which kinds of numbers you find hard: the teens, the
irregular hundreds like `quinientos`, prices with cents, weights. It asks more
questions from the kinds you miss, and anything you get wrong specifically comes
back a few questions later, then again further out, and stops coming back once you
have answered it correctly twice in a row.

You do not have to manage any of this. It is why practice gets harder in the
places that are actually hard for you.

## Daily goal and streaks

The daily goal counts correct answers — 20 by default, adjustable in Settings.
Meet it and the day is stamped on your home screen. Meet it on consecutive days
and you build a streak; miss a day and the streak is over, which the app tells you
plainly rather than showing a number that is no longer true.

Answers count the moment you give them, not when a round ends. Five questions on
one mode, a few more on another, leaving each of them half-played — it all adds
up, and a round you never finish still moves the ring.

The statistics screen shows your streak, your totals, this month's accuracy, and a
bar per skill, so you can see what is weak instead of guessing.

## Settings worth knowing about

- **Number range** — from single digits up to a million. Start small. Changing it
  applies straight away, including to a round you have left part-played: come back
  to it and the question waiting for you is inside the new range.
- **Questions per round** — 10, 20, 30, or endless.
- **Speech speed** — how fast the Spanish is read out. Dragging it plays a sample
  so you can hear the difference. Inside a round there is also a "slower" button
  that halves the speed for that round only.
- **Interface language** — Russian, English or Spanish. The first time you open
  the app it follows your phone's language, falling back to English if your
  language is not one of the three. The numbers you are learning are always
  Spanish (es-ES) whatever the interface says.
- **Accept answers without accents** — on by default, marking them "almost".
- **Answer sounds** — a short tone for right and wrong. On by default.
- **Theme** — automatic, light or dark.

## Your data stays with you

wordavi has no server and no account. Your settings, your progress and your
streak are stored by your browser, on your device. Nothing is uploaded, and there
is no tracking or analytics of any kind.

That has one consequence worth knowing about: clearing your browser data, or
switching to a different phone, means starting fresh. So Settings has **Download
data**, which saves everything to a file, and **Restore from file**, which loads
it back. It is the only way to move your progress to another device — and worth
doing before you ever use "Reset all data".

The single exception to nothing-leaves-your-device is the "Say it out loud" mode.
Speech recognition in a browser sends the audio to your browser vendor's servers
to be transcribed, which is why that mode needs an internet connection. wordavi
itself never keeps what you said.

## If something goes wrong

Settings has **Report a problem**. Describe what happened, attach a screenshot if
it helps, and it gathers the app version, your browser, your settings and a short
log of recent actions so the problem can actually be found. You choose whether to
send it; nothing is transmitted until you do.

If the app ever crashes, it shows a friendly screen with a reload button and the
same report option, rather than a blank page.
